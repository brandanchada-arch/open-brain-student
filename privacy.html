Brain Calendar privacy policy

Last updated: October 7, 2026

Brain Calendar is a personal planning tool. Brandan Chada built it for his own use, and he is its only user. This page explains what data it uses and what happens to it. See also the home page.

Google Calendar data

Brain Calendar connects only to its owner's Google account, with his permission. It asks Google for these permissions:

  • See events on his calendars (calendar.events.readonly), to show his classes and appointments next to his to-do list and to find free time for suggested blocks.
  • See the list of his calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly), to find the "Brain" calendar the app made.
  • Make and change calendars the app creates (calendar.app.created), so it can keep its own "Brain" calendar. It cannot add, change or delete events on any other calendar.

Events from his main calendars are read when they are needed. They are not copied into a database, with two exceptions: the full text of every message the app sends him is stored as a record of what was sent (a morning summary lists the day's events, and a re-plan message can name the event that clashes with a block); and the titles and times of upcoming events that mark quiet time (for example "Prayer") are stored so the app stays quiet even when the calendar can't be read. Brain Calendar keeps its own records of the items it puts on the "Brain" calendar (title, times and the Google event ID) so it can keep the two in step.

Gmail data

Brain Calendar also asks for two Gmail permissions:

  • Read his email (gmail.readonly). In practice the app only looks at messages that carry one of its own Gmail labels ("Brain/Job alerts", "Brain/Jobs", "Brain/GCU" and, if he makes it, "Brain/Watch"). The owner's own Gmail filters add those labels. It reads the list of label names to find them. It never reads attachments.
  • Make drafts (gmail.compose). When an email asks him to pick a time to meet, the app writes a reply draft on that email thread in his Gmail Drafts. Brain Calendar never sends email. He reviews, edits and sends every reply himself. Google's permission also allows sending, but the app's code has no way to send.

What is read: for each labeled email, the sender, the reply-to address, the subject, Gmail's short preview (up to 300 characters), the email's Message-ID header and Gmail's message and thread IDs. The full text is read only for job-alert emails (to find the job openings in them) and for emails that ask to set up a meeting or interview (to find the times on offer); for those, only the newest message is used, not the quoted history.

What is stored in the database: the sender's name and address, the reply-to address, the subject, the short preview (up to 300 characters, blanked after 30 days), the date, Gmail's message and thread IDs, the email's Message-ID header (so a reply draft joins the same conversation), how the email was sorted (for example "class" or "interview"), a one-line summary, the ID of any draft made, and a short technical note if the app couldn't handle the email. For job alerts: each opening's title, company, location, score and a few words on why. The full text of emails is never stored. Messages the app sends him on Telegram about an email (sender, subject and summary) are stored like every other message it sends.

What is sent to AI (OpenRouter, see below), only to sort the email for him: the sender's name and address, the subject and the short preview (up to 300 characters); for job alerts, the subject and the text of the alert with all links removed (up to 6,000 characters), plus his "job profile" setting (a short description he can edit of the jobs he is looking for, up to 800 characters); for emails about setting up a time, the subject and the newest message's text (up to 1,500 characters, no quoted history, no links). A few earlier emails he marked as "should have come through" (sender and subject only) are included as examples. Email IDs, attachments and other mail are never sent.

Brain Calendar's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google Calendar and Gmail data are used only to provide these features to the owner. They are never used for advertising, never sold, never read by people other than the owner, and never used by Brain Calendar to train AI models.

Where data is stored

  • Database: to-do items, settings, plan suggestions, check-in answers, the email details listed above and his job list are stored in a private Supabase database (Supabase hosts the data). Only the owner's account can read his rows.
  • Google sign-in: the refresh token that lets the app reach Google Calendar and Gmail is stored as an encrypted secret in the app's Supabase project settings. It is not stored in the app's code or on GitHub.
  • Telegram: reminders, check-ins and the morning summary are sent to the owner's own Telegram chat through Telegram's service. A morning summary can include the titles and times of that day's calendar events. Notices about important emails and job openings include the sender, subject and a one-line summary.
  • Open Brain notes: the app reads new notes the owner saves in his own Open Brain notes app (in the same database) to find to-dos and things he has finished. It can add new notes there (for example check-in answers and "Done" notes), but never changes or deletes his notes.
  • AI: some text is sent to OpenRouter, an AI service, to sort it. That text is: a new note he saved (up to 1,500 characters), the titles of his open to-do items, his goal names and a few recent notes with whether he said yes or no to scheduling them (to find to-dos); his own answer to a check-in (to tag the reason); and messages he sends the Planner bot to add an item, such as "add: dentist Friday 3pm", or a day and time he types when asking for a different time, such as "Thu 7pm" (to read the date and time); and the email details listed under "Gmail data" above (to sort email and score job openings). Information read from Google Calendar is not sent to AI services.
  • The owner's computer: a weekly check of his school portal (Halo) runs on his own computer and sends assignment due dates and announcements to the database. Grades are not collected.

Sharing

Brain Calendar does not sell, rent or share data with anyone. There are no ads and no analytics trackers. Data goes only to the services listed above, so that the app can work.

Removing data

  • Deleting the "Brain" calendar in Google Calendar removes every event the app added.
  • Removing the "Brain/..." labels in Gmail stops the app from reading those emails. Drafts it made can be deleted in Gmail Drafts like any other draft.
  • Google access can be removed at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • The owner can delete the app's database tables at any time. To ask about any data, email the address below.
