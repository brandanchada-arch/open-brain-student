Brain Calendar privacy policy

Last updated: October 3, 2026

Brain Calendar is a personal planning tool. Brandan Chada built it for his own use, and he is its only user. This page explains what data it uses and what happens to it. See also the home page.

Google Calendar data

Brain Calendar connects only to its owner's Google account, with his permission. It asks Google for these permissions:

  • See events on his calendars (calendar.events.readonly), to show his classes and appointments next to his to-do list and to find free time for suggested blocks.
  • See the list of his calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly), to find the "Brain" calendar the app made.
  • Make and change calendars the app creates (calendar.app.created), so it can keep its own "Brain" calendar. It cannot add, change or delete events on any other calendar.

Events from his main calendars are read when they are needed. They are not copied into a database, with two exceptions: the full text of every message the app sends him is stored as a record of what was sent (a morning summary lists the day's events, and a re-plan message can name the event that clashes with a block); and the titles and times of upcoming events that mark quiet time (for example "Prayer") are stored so the app stays quiet even when the calendar can't be read. Brain Calendar keeps its own records of the items it puts on the "Brain" calendar (title, times and the Google event ID) so it can keep the two in step.

Brain Calendar's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google Calendar data is never used for advertising, never sold, and never used to train AI models.

Where data is stored

  • Database: to-do items, settings, plan suggestions and check-in answers are stored in a private Supabase database (Supabase hosts the data). Only the owner's account can read his rows.
  • Google sign-in: the refresh token that lets the app reach Google Calendar is stored as an encrypted secret in the app's Supabase project settings. It is not stored in the app's code or on GitHub.
  • Telegram: reminders, check-ins and the morning summary are sent to the owner's own Telegram chat through Telegram's service. A morning summary can include the titles and times of that day's calendar events.
  • Open Brain notes: the app reads new notes the owner saves in his own Open Brain notes app (in the same database) to find to-dos and things he has finished. It can add new notes there (for example check-in answers and "Done" notes), but never changes or deletes his notes.
  • AI: some text is sent to OpenRouter, an AI service, to sort it. That text is: a new note he saved (up to 1,500 characters), the titles of his open to-do items, his goal names and a few recent notes with whether he said yes or no to scheduling them (to find to-dos); his own answer to a check-in (to tag the reason); and messages he sends the Planner bot to add an item, such as "add: dentist Friday 3pm", or a day and time he types when asking for a different time, such as "Thu 7pm" (to read the date and time). Information read from Google Calendar is not sent to AI services.
  • The owner's computer: a weekly check of his school portal (Halo) runs on his own computer and sends assignment due dates and announcements to the database. Grades are not collected.

Sharing

Brain Calendar does not sell, rent or share data with anyone. There are no ads and no analytics trackers. Data goes only to the services listed above, so that the app can work.

Removing data

  • Deleting the "Brain" calendar in Google Calendar removes every event the app added.
  • Google access can be removed at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • The owner can delete the app's database tables at any time. To ask about any data, email the address below.
